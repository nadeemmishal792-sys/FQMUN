FQMUN 2026 · COMMITTEE 02

UNSC — Committee Study Guide

A practical handbook for delegates preparing for Security Council debate, diplomacy, sanctions, crisis response and resolution drafting.

Core mindset: The UNSC is not a normal debate. Think in terms of mandate, security consequences, feasibility, coalition arithmetic and implementation.

01 · COUNCIL MANDATE

Know what the Security Council can do

  • Article 24 gives the Council primary responsibility for international peace and security.
  • Article 25 concerns Member States accepting and carrying out Security Council decisions.
  • Article 39 concerns determining the existence of a threat to the peace, breach of the peace or act of aggression.
  • Articles 41 and 42 cover measures not involving armed force and, where necessary, action involving armed force.
  • Article 51 recognizes the inherent right of individual or collective self-defence.

Know the difference between a political recommendation and a binding Council decision, and verify the exact language of any resolution you cite.

02 · P5 & VETO

Understand Council arithmetic

Permanent members

China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States hold permanent seats and veto power over substantive resolutions.

Non-permanent members

Ten elected members participate in Council work and can be decisive in building the required support for action.

Voting strategy

Do not write a resolution that cannot survive the Council's political arithmetic. Negotiate wording before the final vote.

Veto awareness

A veto is a political tool, not a substitute for diplomacy. Anticipate objections and design language that can attract broader support.

03 · CHAPTER VI & VII

Peaceful settlement vs enforcement

  • Chapter VI focuses on peaceful settlement of disputes.
  • Chapter VII provides the framework for action regarding threats to peace, breaches of peace and acts of aggression.
  • Sanctions proposals should identify targets, exemptions, humanitarian safeguards and monitoring.
  • Peacekeeping and peace enforcement are not interchangeable concepts; understand mandate, consent and rules of engagement.

04 · CRISIS DIPLOMACY

When the situation changes

  • Separate verified facts from rumours and propaganda.
  • Identify the immediate security threat, affected civilians and regional spillover.
  • Propose an action that the Council can realistically authorize or recommend.
  • Keep escalation risks in mind: military action can create consequences beyond the immediate crisis.
  • Use allies to gather information and negotiate language before publicly committing to a position.
Crisis response formula: Assess → verify → prioritize civilian/security risk → consult allies → propose action → negotiate safeguards → implement → monitor.

05 · SPEAKING

Build Security Council speeches

GSL

Threat → national position → evidence → Council responsibility → 2–3 actions → coalition invitation.

Moderated caucus

Address the exact security issue. Explain the consequence, legal basis and operational solution.

POI

Challenge feasibility, mandate, civilian safeguards, funding, verification or unintended consequences.

Rebuttal

Do not attack the country personally. Attack the weakness in the policy and replace it with a workable alternative.

06 · RESOLUTION DRAFTING

Write clauses the Council can use

  • Use operative verbs appropriate to the Council's authority and the nature of the draft.
  • Define the implementing actor: UN body, Member States, sanctions committee, monitoring mechanism or other relevant institution.
  • Include timelines, reporting requirements, humanitarian exemptions and review mechanisms where appropriate.
  • Connect every major demand to the problem identified in the preambulatory section.
  • Negotiate ambiguous language before final voting.
Operational test: authority + actor + action + timeline + safeguards + reporting + review.

07 · AWARD STRATEGY

Be more than a speaker

  • Bring credible research and legal references.
  • Speak strategically rather than excessively.
  • Build cross-bloc agreements.
  • Improve the draft resolution instead of only criticizing it.
  • Stay calm during crises and help the committee move forward.

08 · FINAL CHECKLIST

  • Country foreign-policy position mapped
  • UN Charter provisions verified
  • Relevant Security Council resolutions researched
  • P5 and likely bloc positions identified
  • 90-second GSL prepared
  • 10 POIs and 10 rebuttals prepared
  • Sanctions/crisis options evaluated
  • Draft operative clauses prepared
  • Harvard-style source list completed
