FQMUN 2026 · COMMITTEE 01

PNA — Committee Study Guide

A complete preparation handbook for delegates representing Pakistani political personalities in the Pakistan National Assembly simulation.

Goal: Do not memorize speeches. Build a political position, defend it, negotiate intelligently and turn arguments into workable parliamentary action.

01 · KNOW YOUR ROLE

Personality research

  • Build a one-page profile: offices held, party affiliation, political era, major policies, allies, opponents, controversies and public record.
  • Identify what your personality would realistically support, reject or negotiate on the agenda.
  • Separate historical fact from personal opinion. Never invent quotations, positions or legislation.
  • Prepare five evidence-backed talking points, five likely attacks and five responses.
Personality formula: Past record → political ideology → constituency interests → policy position → coalition choices.

02 · CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Pakistan parliamentary foundations

Study the Constitution of Pakistan relevant to your agenda. Pay particular attention to fundamental rights, Parliament, the federal structure, law-making and constitutional limits. Use the exact constitutional provision only when it supports your argument.

Articles to know

  • Article 4 — right to be dealt with in accordance with law
  • Articles 8–28 — fundamental rights framework
  • Article 25 — equality of citizens
  • Article 26 — non-discrimination in access to public places
  • Article 27 — safeguards as to services
  • Articles 50–89 — Parliament and legislative procedure

Research checklist

  • Current or historical law connected to the agenda
  • Relevant ministry and implementing institution
  • Budget and implementation constraints
  • Federal/provincial responsibility
  • Existing gaps and measurable reforms

03 · PARLIAMENTARY PROCEDURE

Master the floor

  • Know how the Chair recognizes speakers and controls debate.
  • Prepare motions for moderated/unmoderated discussion only according to the conference rules announced by the Secretariat.
  • Understand points of order, personal privilege, parliamentary inquiry and clarification if included in FQMUN procedure.
  • During drafting, distinguish policy ideas from formal bill language.
  • Never interrupt a recognized speaker unless the rules expressly permit it.

04 · SPEAKING

GSL and moderated caucus

60–90 second GSL

Hook → personality's position → evidence → problem → 2–3 solutions → coalition invitation.

Moderated caucus

Answer the exact subtopic. Make one strong claim, support it with evidence, propose one implementable action and end with a diplomatic call.

POI

Ask a precise question that exposes a policy gap. Avoid speeches disguised as questions.

Rebuttal

Acknowledge the concern → identify the flaw → give evidence → present the alternative.

05 · DRAFTING

Turn debate into legislation

  • State the problem clearly before proposing a clause.
  • Specify implementing authority, target group, timeline, funding mechanism and monitoring.
  • Use measurable language: establish, amend, require, allocate, publish, review, monitor.
  • Check that every proposal is constitutionally and administratively plausible.
  • Build amendments through negotiation rather than treating the first draft as final.
Clause test: Who acts? What do they do? By when? With what resources? How is compliance measured?

06 · COALITION & AWARDS

How to become indispensable

  • Find delegates who share your solution, not necessarily your entire political ideology.
  • Bring useful clauses to the bloc and improve other delegates' ideas.
  • Speak consistently but do not dominate every topic.
  • Track promises, amendments and votes.
  • Help produce a coherent final bill/resolution.

Award-level delegate = research + speaking + negotiation + drafting + diplomacy + sustained contribution.

07 · FINAL CHECKLIST

  • Personality profile completed
  • Agenda research and credible sources collected
  • Constitutional provisions verified
  • 90-second GSL prepared
  • At least 6 caucus speeches outlined
  • 10 POIs and 10 rebuttals prepared
  • 3–5 draft policy clauses ready
  • Potential allies and opposition mapped
  • Position paper and Harvard-style references checked
