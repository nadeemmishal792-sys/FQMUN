FQMUN 2026 · COMMITTEE 03

UNHRC — Committee Study Guide

A complete preparation handbook for delegates debating human rights, equality, accountability and social justice.

Core mindset: Human-rights diplomacy combines law, evidence, lived impact, state responsibility and realistic implementation. Strong rhetoric must be backed by credible sources.

01 · HUMAN RIGHTS FOUNDATION

Know the legal framework

UDHR

  • Article 1 — dignity and equality
  • Article 2 — non-discrimination
  • Article 7 — equality before the law

ICCPR

  • Article 2 — respect and ensure rights without discrimination
  • Article 26 — equality before the law and equal protection

Pakistan Constitution

  • Article 25 — equality of citizens
  • Article 26 — non-discrimination in access to public places
  • Article 27 — safeguards as to services
  • Article 37 — promotion of social justice and removal of social evils

Always verify the exact treaty, constitutional or statutory language before quoting it in committee.

02 · UNHRC MECHANISMS

Understand the Council's tools

  • Universal Periodic Review (UPR): review of the human-rights record of every UN Member State through an intergovernmental process.
  • Special Procedures: independent experts and working groups examine thematic or country-specific human-rights situations.
  • Commissions of inquiry / fact-finding mechanisms: can investigate serious allegations when established through the relevant UN process.
  • Resolutions: express the Council's position and recommendations; understand the difference between political recommendation and domestic legal enforcement.

03 · EVIDENCE & ACCOUNTABILITY

Build an evidence-based case

  • Use UN reports, treaty texts, constitutional provisions, legislation, official statistics and reputable institutional research.
  • Distinguish allegation, documented finding and verified official data.
  • Identify who has responsibility: government department, provincial authority, police, courts, employers, schools, civil society or international partners.
  • Recommend monitoring, reporting and accountability mechanisms that match the problem.
Evidence formula: claim → credible source → affected population → legal standard → accountability gap → implementable recommendation.

04 · PAKISTAN-FOCUSED PREPARATION

Turn the agenda into policy

For the FQMUN Pakistan-focused agenda, research both progress and remaining gaps. Review relevant national laws and institutions, including legislation addressing violence, child protection, juvenile justice, workplace harassment and disability rights where relevant to your subtopic.

  • Identify the legal protection already available.
  • Explain the implementation gap instead of simply claiming that no law exists.
  • Recommend capacity building, access to justice, reporting systems, awareness, data collection and institutional coordination where appropriate.
  • Keep proposals realistic for Pakistan's constitutional and administrative structure.

05 · SPEAKING

Human-rights diplomacy on the floor

GSL

Human story → legal principle → evidence → national position → 3 concrete recommendations → cooperation.

Moderated caucus

Define the rights problem, cite the standard, identify the gap and propose one measurable action.

POI

Ask about legal compliance, implementation, evidence, vulnerable groups, monitoring or feasibility.

Rebuttal

Correct the factual/legal weakness respectfully and offer a stronger rights-based alternative.

06 · RESOLUTION DRAFTING

Make recommendations implementable

  • Name the responsible institution or actor.
  • Define the target population and geographic scope.
  • Include training, funding or capacity support where required.
  • Require transparent reporting and measurable indicators.
  • Build review periods so the committee can assess progress.
  • Avoid vague language such as “solve the issue immediately.”
Recommendation test: Who implements it? Who benefits? What changes? What resources are needed? How will progress be measured?

07 · POSITION PAPER & RESEARCH

Harvard-style evidence

  1. Committee, agenda, country and delegate
  2. Problem definition and significance
  3. Country position
  4. Previous national and international action
  5. Legal framework
  6. Implementation gaps
  7. Three to five solutions
  8. Implementation and monitoring
  9. Conclusion
  10. Harvard-style references

08 · AWARD STRATEGY

What distinguishes a strong UNHRC delegate

  • Accurate legal references instead of decorative citations.
  • Human impact explained without exploiting victims for rhetoric.
  • Constructive engagement with different national positions.
  • Useful drafting and coalition building.
  • Consistent, evidence-backed contributions throughout the conference.

09 · FINAL CHECKLIST

  • UDHR and ICCPR provisions relevant to agenda verified
  • Pakistan constitutional/legal framework researched
  • Relevant UN mechanisms understood
  • Country position mapped
  • 90-second GSL prepared
  • 10 POIs and 10 rebuttals prepared
  • 6+ moderated-caucus topics outlined
  • 3–5 implementable clauses prepared
  • Credible Harvard-style source list completed
